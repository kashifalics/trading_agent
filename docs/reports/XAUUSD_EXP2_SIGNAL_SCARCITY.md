# XAUUSD experiment 2: signal-scarcity diagnostic

_Generated by `python -m app.gold_research.exp2 diagnose` from `data/historical/research/XAUUSD/exp2/diagnostics.json` (counted 2026-11-09). **Counts only:** no trade outcome, profitability statistic or parameter was computed, tested or changed; experiment 2's stored results and its report are unchanged. Training period only; the sealed final test was not read. Analysis only._

## Summary

1. **7 of 36 tests reached the 150-trade minimum** (H4 5/12, H8 2/12, D1 0/12). Development trades per test: H4 21-483, H8 9-256, D1 3-127, over 11.7 development years.
2. **Most of the scarcity comes from the setup and test design, not from defects.** In order of effect: the timeframe (the training period has 26,957 H4 candles, 13,929 H8 and 5,224 D1); S6's two conditions that partly exclude each other; S3's 2-ATR displacement candle (85% of 3-candle gaps removed on H4); S2's BOS-only rule (46% of structure breaks removed); S1's higher-timeframe filter (45% of zones removed); S5's limit of one sweep per side per week, plus its first-touch rule (45% of reclaim week-sides removed); the 20-candle limit wait (26% of H4 development orders expire untouched); and testing long and short separately, which halves every sample.
3. **Execution and sample filters cost little.** The conservative fill rule removes 0-6% of touched orders; the gap rule removes 150 of 4,045 filled development trades (4%); development-boundary exits remove 0; cancellations before a fill: 1.
4. **Defects found:**
   * daily-break gaps are counted as missing data (35 development trades lost);
   * S4 refreshes its levels every 200 candles on every timeframe, which leaves the levels stale on H8 and D1;
   * S4 picks an arbitrary band when one candle breaks several;
   * the cancel rule is effectively inert;
   * the gap list is not limited to the permitted period.
   Their effect on counts is small, and none would bring any test to 150 by itself (section 9).
5. **The 150 minimum was too low to give the tests statistical power, and too high to reach on H8 and D1.** At 150 trades the smallest edge detectable with 80% power is about 0.29R (0.44R at the 36-test level); the deflated-Sharpe criterion over 1,967 trials needs a mean of about 0.43R at 150 trades. At the observed rates, D1 tests would need 14-583 years of data to reach 150, and H8 tests other than S4 would need 13-194 years.

## 8. Was the 150-trade minimum realistic?

The power figures below assume a 2R/-1R outcome at the break-even win rate of one in three, an SD of 1.41R. Time stops make the real SD somewhat lower (with an SD of 1.2R, every detectable edge shrinks by 15% and every required sample by about 28%). Overlapping trades and week clustering make it effectively higher. Power is 80%, and the test is one-sided.

| Significance level | Smallest detectable edge at 150 trades | Trades needed for 0.10R | Trades needed for 0.15R |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 0.29R | 1,237 | 550 |
| 0.05 / 36 (the 36 tests) | 0.44R | 2,939 | 1,306 |

**The deflated-Sharpe criterion is stricter still.** It counts 1,967 trials (experiments 1 and 2), and the code needs a per-trade Sharpe of about 0.31 at 150 trades, 0.27 at 300 trades, 0.24 at 500 trades, 0.22 at 1,000 trades, which at the assumed SD is a mean of about 0.43R at 150, 0.38R at 300, 0.35R at 500, 0.32R at 1,000. This is a normal approximation from sample sizes only.

Part of this hurdle does not shrink with sample size. The criterion asks whether a test beats the best of 1,967 trials by luck, so it needs a per-trade Sharpe above 0.17 (about 0.24R at the assumed SD) even with unlimited trades. More trades alone cannot get a small edge past it; fewer total trials can, and every new experiment raises it.

## 9. Implementation defects and inconsistencies (none fixed; experiment 2's results are unchanged)

**D1. Daily-break gaps counted as missing data.** Of the 642 unscheduled gaps in training, 79 start at 21:00-21:05 or 22:00-22:05 UTC (the daily rollover break) and last up to 2 hours. By year: 2003 34, 2004 7, 2005 1, 2013 2, 2014 31, 2015 2, 2017 2.
* **What they are:** recurring daily closures that ran longer, or at a different hour, than the calendar assumes (for example in US summer time in 2014). They are not feed failures; prices exist on both sides, and the simulator already handles a stop minute that opens beyond the stop.
* **Effect:** 35 development trades were excluded only because of them (H4 17, H8 10, D1 8), at most 6 in any one test.
* **Code:** `exp2/data.py:46-57`.

**D2. The S4 level refresh is fixed at 200 candles on every timeframe.** That is about 6 weeks on H4, 13 weeks on H8 and 8 months on D1.
* **Why it matters:** levels that form inside a window are ignored until the next refresh, and a window with no band near price yields no breakouts.
* **Effect:** D1 has no S4 breakout at all in 2010. Refreshing at the same calendar interval would give H8 802 instead of 675 development breakouts, and D1 321 instead of 250.
* **Origin:** inherited from experiment 1, which was built for M5-H4. Code: `app/gold_research/events.py:36, 65-79`.

**D3. S4 picks an arbitrary band when one candle breaks several.**
* **Where it happens:** H4 177, H8 84, D1 25 development candles broke two or more bands.
* **What the code does:** it keeps the first band in the list (`run.py:126`, `unique(..., keep="first")`). Levels are sorted by distance from the price at the window start (`levels.py:96`), not from the breakout candle.
* **Effect:** this changes the limit price of those orders, not the count beyond the deduplication.

**D4. The cancel rule is effectively inert.** Only 1 of 5,593 development orders was cancelled before a touch.
* **Why:** for a long, the far edge lies below the limit, so a candle cannot close below the far edge without first trading through the limit. Only a price gap, or a spread wider than the zone, can skip it.
* **Effect:** orders that slice through the whole zone are filled and then usually stopped, which is realistic for a resting limit order. The documentation ("cancelled if a candle closes beyond the far edge first") overstates what the rule does; counts are unaffected.

**D5. A documentation detail: which spread the conservative fill uses.** The code uses the signal candle's *opening* spread (`run.py:132`). This was checked and is harmless: the median opening spread at signals matches the all-candle median (H4 0.36 vs 0.37, H8 0.36 vs 0.36, D1 0.40 vs 0.40). Opening spreads above 3x the median occur at H4 0.14%, H8 0.08%, D1 0.25% of signals. Switching to the closing spread or the median spread changes fills by at most a few per test (section 4).

**D6. The gap list is not limited to the permitted period.**
* **What it covers:** `exp2/data.py::gaps_v2` loads the missing-data runs (start time and length only, no prices) for the whole dataset. Trades only look up gaps inside training.
* **Disclosure:** during this diagnostic, one exploratory query printed, for every year including the sealed years 2023-2025, the number of unscheduled gaps (one or two a year), their median length and the date of the last one (November 2025). No price, spread or trade data from the sealed period was read. The diagnostic's own gap counts are limited to training.
* **Fix:** filter the gap list to the permitted period at load time.

**D7. One S1 condition never removes an event.** "Closes beyond the base in the impulse direction" is always met once the range, body and base conditions hold. It is redundant, not harmful.

**Combined effect:** none of D1-D7 would bring any test to 150 by itself. Only design changes would (section 12).

## 10. Hypotheses checked and rejected

* *"Wide rollover spreads at the signal candle's open make conservative fills rare."* Rejected (D5): the conservative rule costs 0-6% of touched orders.
* *"The W1 trend is usually neutral, so D1 S1 rarely aligns."* Rejected: W1 was neutral at 1.0% of D1 zones. The filter removes about half the zones because it matches the direction against a trend that is almost always up or down.
* *"Trades are lost at the development boundary."* Rejected: 0 lost.
* *"Limit orders are cancelled before filling."* Rejected: 1 cancellation.

## 11. Limitations

* **Counts only.** Whether a looser rule would produce better or worse trades is unknown and was deliberately not examined. Fewer filters usually mean more trades of lower average quality.
* **The S1-S3 funnels are re-counts.** They re-implement the detectors' conditions step by step; the detector's own output (the "emitted" rows) is what the experiment used. They agree to within one event (for example S1 on H4: 689 re-counted, 688 emitted).
* **The power figures are approximations.** They rest on an assumed outcome SD, a normal approximation and no clustering adjustment.
* **These counts came after experiment 2's results.** They were made after its development results (no candidates) were known. Using counts to judge feasibility does not use outcomes, but any new experiment designed now is still post hoc for 2003-2020 and needs fresh data to validate.
* **The multiple-testing family over-counts S5.** The three S5 timeframe tests largely share the same weekly events, so they are not independent hypotheses.

## 12. Recommendations for a possible future experiment (not started; needs your approval)

1. **Set a power requirement before any outcome is seen, in place of the fixed 150 minimum.** Count events without outcomes, as here, and keep only setup/timeframe pairs that can meet it. For example, detecting 0.15R at significance 0.05 needs about 550 trades per test.
2. **Reduce the number of tests rather than loosening thresholds.**
   * Test long and short together: the direction-matched controls already account for gold's drift. This doubles each sample and halves the number of tests.
   * Test S5 once, on one timeframe.
   * Each test dropped also lowers the deflated-Sharpe hurdle.
3. **Use H4, or H1 entries with H4/D1 context (the brief's multi-timeframe profiles), for setup tests.** Keep D1 and W1 as context only: at D1, these setups produced 0.3-10.9 development trades a year per direction.
4. **Fix D1, D2, D3 and D6 before any re-run:**
   * classify daily-break gaps as scheduled;
   * refresh levels on a calendar interval;
   * pick the band nearest the breakout;
   * limit the gap list to the permitted period.
   Experiment 2's recorded results stay as they are.
5. **Treat wider thresholds as new hypotheses.** Examples: S3 at 1.5 ATR, S1 without the trend filter, S2 including CHoCH, S5 with any-candle reclaims, a longer wait. Each must be pre-registered and added to the trial count. Redefine S6 or drop it.
6. **Validation needs untouched data.**
   * 2015-2020 was screened in this experiment.
   * 2020-2022 was experiment 1's validation period.
   * The sealed 2023-2026 test can be used once, with your approval, for one frozen, pre-registered candidate list, followed by forward paper trading.
